## Which BandwagonHost Plan Should You Buy?

The right choice depends more on the workload than on the plan name.

### For a small website or learning project

Start with the 20G or 40G standard KVM only if the application is genuinely small. A static site, lightweight reverse proxy, basic monitoring server, or short-term development environment may fit.

For WordPress, a small database-backed application, or several Docker containers, the 80G KVM is a more comfortable starting point because it provides 4 GB of RAM and 3 TB of transfer. The extra memory usually matters more than having a large amount of unused storage.

### For a normal production website

The 80G or 160G standard KVM plans are the most sensible range for many small businesses and independent developers.

Choose 80G when:

- You host one or a few modest websites.
- Traffic is predictable.
- You do not need a specialist network route.
- You are comfortable managing Linux yourself.

Choose 160G when:

- You need more room for databases, caching, containers, or background jobs.
- You expect traffic growth.
- You want to avoid upgrading immediately after installation.
- The application is more than a simple brochure site.

### For users who need China-oriented routing

Look at the Singapore, Osaka, Hong Kong, Tokyo, or CN2 GIA Ecommerce categories. Location and route matter here. A server with more RAM in the wrong region may perform worse for your audience than a smaller server in a more suitable location.

Do not assume that every “CN2” plan will produce the same result for every ISP or destination. The product pages describe particular routes and transit arrangements, but real-world latency depends on the user’s network, time of day, destination, and current routing conditions.

### For higher-volume Ecommerce workloads

The Los Angeles Ecommerce SLA plans are the clearest fit when you need NVMe storage, ECC memory, dedicated AMD CPU allocations, stronger network capacity, and a stated 99.99% SLA.

The 20G and 40G plans are relatively modest despite the Ecommerce label. The 80G and 160G products are more realistic starting points for a serious application, provided that you can manage the system yourself.

### For large transfer requirements

The HIBW products are aimed at high-bandwidth workloads. They provide more monthly transfer while retaining 64 GB of RAM, 1,280 GB of storage, and 10 Gbps networking.

That does not automatically make them suitable for every media, download, or streaming application. You still need to review acceptable-use rules, software requirements, backup strategy, and the cost of storing and serving large files.

## Important Things to Check Before Paying

### Self-management

BandwagonHost describes these products as self-managed. That means support should not be confused with server administration. You are expected to understand enough Linux, networking, SSH, firewall rules, and application maintenance to operate the service.

If you want a hosting provider to install, secure, update, and troubleshoot your application for you, compare managed hosting instead.

### Backup scope

The order page lists automatic backups and snapshots on many products. Still, confirm what is included, how often backups run, how long they are retained, and whether restoration has limitations.

A provider-side backup is not a complete disaster-recovery plan. Keep an independent copy of important databases, media, configuration files, and secrets.

### Billing and renewal

The displayed amount may vary by billing period. Before confirming payment, check:

- The selected billing cycle
- The exact data center
- Whether the plan is promotional
- The renewal amount
- Taxes or payment-processing charges
- Whether the selected location can be migrated later
- Whether the product supports the operating system you need

The cart displays different prices for monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, and annual billing, so comparing only the headline monthly number can be misleading.

### Data-center choice

Choose the location based on your users, database dependencies, and external services. A server in Asia may reduce latency for an Asian audience but add latency for users in North America or Europe. The closest physical location is not always the fastest route, but it is a useful starting assumption when you have no measurements.

### IPv6 support

Most standard KVM and CN2 GIA products list routed IPv6 support. The Dubai Ecommerce listings explicitly state that IPv6 is not supported. If IPv6 is part of your deployment plan, verify this before ordering rather than discovering the limitation after migration.
